## What this question wants

Paper 1, Question 5 (40 marks — the biggest single question on the paper)
gives you either a picture or a short written prompt, and asks you to write
a **description** or a **story**. You choose one.

It's marked out of 40: **24 marks for content and organisation** (AO5) and
**16 marks for technical accuracy** (AO6) — spelling, punctuation, grammar,
sentence variety and vocabulary. That split means technical accuracy is
worth more here than it might feel like while you're writing.

## Planning (spend ~5 minutes)

- Decide: description or story? A story needs a shape (beginning/middle/
  end); a description just needs a focus and a structure for moving through
  it (see below) — often quicker to control well under time pressure.
- Plan a **structure**, not just ideas. A few reliable shapes:
  - **Description:** move through the scene in a clear order — wide to
    narrow, far to near, or one sense to another (sight → sound → smell).
  - **Narrative:** a single, contained moment rather than a whole life
    story. A short story that covers five minutes in real depth beats one
    that races through a whole day.
- Jot down 3-4 ambitious vocabulary choices or techniques before you start,
  so you're not hunting for them mid-sentence.

## Techniques to aim for

- **Varied sentence lengths** — short sentences for impact, longer ones to
  build atmosphere or detail.
- **A range of sentence openers** — not every sentence starting with "The"
  or "I".
- **Sensory detail** — don't just describe what's seen; sound, smell,
  touch and temperature make a scene feel real.
- **Figurative language used deliberately** — one well-placed metaphor beats
  five scattered similes.
- **Paragraphing that matches structure** — a new paragraph for a shift in
  focus, time, or mood.

> **Exam tip:** Examiners are told to reward ambition even if it's not
> perfectly executed. A slightly risky metaphor that mostly works is better
> than a safe, flat sentence.

> **Exam tip:** Proofread your last two minutes. SPaG is 16 of the 40 marks
> here — more than almost any other single skill on the whole exam.

## Self-marking your practice answers

For writing questions there's no single "correct" answer, so you mark your
own against band descriptors (shown with each practice prompt): read the
level descriptions, decide honestly which band your writing best matches,
and note one specific thing to improve next time.
