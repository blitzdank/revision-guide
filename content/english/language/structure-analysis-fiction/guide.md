## What this question wants

Paper 1, Question 3 (8 marks) asks how the writer has **structured** the
whole source to interest the reader — not individual words or phrases
(that's Q2), but the bigger choices: what the writer shows you first, what
they hold back, what shifts as the extract goes on.

## Things to look for

- **Opening and closing** — how does the extract begin, and how does it
  end? Writers often open with something intriguing and end on a twist,
  question, or image that lingers.
- **Shifts in focus** — does the writer move from wide/general to
  close-up/specific (or the other way)? From one character to another? From
  description to action or dialogue?
- **Time** — flashback, flashforward, a sudden jump, or time slowing down
  at a key moment.
- **Withholding information** — what does the writer deliberately not tell
  you yet, to build tension or curiosity?
- **Contrast** — a calm moment followed by a sudden one, or two settings/
  characters placed side by side to highlight a difference.
- **Repetition / patterns across the extract** — an image, phrase or idea
  that returns and develops.

## Structuring your answer

Think of the extract in three parts: beginning, middle, end. For each part
you discuss, explain:

1. **What** the writer does structurally (a shift, a withholding, a
   contrast).
2. **Why** — what effect does it have on the reader's interest or
   understanding at that point?

> **Exam tip:** Don't retell the plot. "First X happens, then Y happens" is
> a summary, not structural analysis. You need to say *why* the writer chose
> to order or focus it that way.

> **Exam tip:** A strong Q3 answer often tracks *change* across the extract
> — how the reader's feeling or understanding shifts from the opening to
> the close.

## Worked example

If an extract opens with a wide description of an empty street, then
narrows to a single lit window, then ends with a close-up on a hand
reaching for a door handle — that's a classic structural move: wide to
narrow, general to specific, building a sense of being drawn in and
creating suspense about what's behind the door.
