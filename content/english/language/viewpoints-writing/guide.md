## What this question wants

Paper 2, Question 5 (40 marks) asks you to write to express a **viewpoint**
— usually a letter, article, speech or similar, arguing a point of view on
a statement you're given (e.g. "Social media does more harm than good.
Write an article for a newspaper arguing your point of view.").

Like Paper 1's Q5, it's marked out of 40: **24 for content and organisation**
(AO5), **16 for technical accuracy** (AO6).

## Planning (spend ~5 minutes)

- Decide your **position** — you don't have to fully agree or disagree;
  a nuanced view ("mostly, but...") can work well if you argue it clearly.
- Plan **3-4 distinct points**, each with a reason or example — don't just
  restate the same idea in different words.
- Think about **form**: does it need a greeting/sign-off (letter), a
  headline and byline (article), direct address to an audience (speech)?
  Match your tone and structure to the form you're given.

## Techniques for persuasive/viewpoint writing

- **Rhetorical questions** — used sparingly, to make the reader pause and
  consider.
- **Direct address** — "you," "we" — draws the reader in and makes the
  argument feel personal.
- **Rule of three** — grouping ideas or examples in threes for rhythm and
  emphasis.
- **Counter-argument and rebuttal** — briefly acknowledging the other side,
  then showing why your view still stands. This is one of the strongest
  ways to show a controlled, convincing argument.
- **Varied register** — matching formality to the form (a speech can be
  more emotive; a formal letter needs more restraint).

> **Exam tip:** A one-sided rant that never acknowledges the other side
> rarely scores as highly as an argument that briefly considers — and
> dismisses — a counter-argument.

> **Exam tip:** Don't let technique choice get in the way of a clear line
> of argument. Examiners reward a well-organised, convincing viewpoint over
> a list of persuasive devices with no real argument behind them.

## Self-marking your practice answers

As with descriptive/narrative writing, mark your own response against the
level descriptors given with each prompt, and note one specific thing to
improve.
