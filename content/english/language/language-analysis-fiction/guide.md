## What this question wants

Paper 1, Question 2 (8 marks) gives you a short section of the unseen fiction
extract and asks how the writer uses **language** to describe something or
create an effect — for example a setting, a character, or a mood.

This is not a "find the technique" scavenger hunt. The marks are for
explaining the **effect** the language has on the reader, with the technique
as your evidence, not your point.

## Techniques worth knowing

- **Simile** — comparing two things using "like" or "as" ("the sky was like
  a bruise").
- **Metaphor** — describing one thing *as if it were* another, without
  "like"/"as" ("the city was a furnace").
- **Personification** — giving human qualities to something non-human ("the
  wind screamed through the alley").
- **Pathetic fallacy** — a specific kind of personification where weather or
  nature reflects a character's mood or the story's atmosphere.
- **Sibilance** — repeated "s" sounds, often creating a hissing, unsettling
  effect.
- **Alliteration** — repeated consonant sounds at the start of nearby words,
  used for emphasis or rhythm.
- **Connotation** — the feelings/associations a word carries beyond its
  literal meaning (e.g. "slender" vs "skinny" — similar meaning, very
  different connotation).
- **Word classes** — picking out a verb, adjective or noun and explaining
  *why that specific word* was chosen over a more ordinary alternative.

## Structuring your answer

Use a clear point → evidence → effect pattern for each technique you pick:

1. **Point** — name the technique and what it's doing.
2. **Evidence** — a short, precise quotation (don't quote whole sentences).
3. **Effect** — what does it make the reader feel, see, or think? Why that
   word/image specifically?

> **Exam tip:** Two well-explained points beat four technique-spotted ones.
> If you can't explain the *effect*, the technique-spotting alone won't get
> you far up the mark scheme.

> **Exam tip:** Zoom in on individual words, not whole sentences. "Shattered"
> is more revealing to analyse than "the glass shattered loudly on the
> floor" as a whole quotation.

## Worked example

Extract line: *"The old house crouched at the end of the lane, its windows
like hollow eyes."*

- **Point:** The writer personifies the house as a living, watchful thing.
- **Evidence:** "crouched" and "windows like hollow eyes."
- **Effect:** "Crouched" suggests the house is poised, almost predatory,
  immediately unsettling the reader before anything has happened. The simile
  "hollow eyes" strips the house of any warmth or life, making it feel
  abandoned and watchful at once — foreshadowing something unsettling inside.
