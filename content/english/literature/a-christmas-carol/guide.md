## The exam question

Paper 1, Section B works the same way as the Shakespeare question: a
printed extract from the novel, plus a question requiring you to write
about the **whole novel** too (30 marks, no separate AO4 marks here — that's
only on the Shakespeare and modern-text questions).

## Plot in brief

Ebenezer Scrooge, a cold, miserly businessman, is visited on Christmas Eve
by the ghost of his dead business partner Jacob Marley, who warns him that
three more spirits will visit. The **Ghost of Christmas Past** shows
Scrooge his lonely childhood and the choices that hardened him. The **Ghost
of Christmas Present** shows him the Cratchit family's poverty-stricken but
loving Christmas (including Tiny Tim, who may die without help) and his
nephew Fred's warmth, which Scrooge has always rejected. The **Ghost of
Christmas Yet to Come** shows a bleak future where Scrooge dies unmourned
and his possessions are picked over. Terrified, Scrooge wakes transformed:
generous, warm, and determined to "honour Christmas" all year round.

## Key themes

- **Redemption and transformation** — the whole novel is structured around
  Scrooge's change; Dickens wants to show change is possible for anyone.
- **Social responsibility** — Dickens, writing in 1843, directly criticises
  Victorian attitudes to poverty (the Poor Law, workhouses) through
  characters like the Portly Gentlemen and Scrooge's early views ("are
  there no workhouses?").
- **Family and generosity** — the Cratchits have little money but real
  warmth; contrasted directly with Scrooge's wealth and isolation.
- **The supernatural as a moral device** — the ghosts aren't there to scare
  for their own sake; each one forces Scrooge to confront a different
  relationship to time and responsibility.
- **Isolation vs community** — Scrooge's self-imposed isolation (turning
  down Fred's dinner invite, having no friends) is presented as both cause
  and consequence of his miserliness.

## Key quotations (public domain — quote these directly)

- *"Bah! Humbug!"* (Scrooge, Stave 1) — his dismissal of Christmas/warmth,
  established immediately.
- *"Are there no prisons? ... And the Union workhouses?"* (Scrooge, Stave
  1) — his cold, dehumanising view of poverty, used by Dickens as direct
  social criticism.
- *"Mankind was my business"* (Marley's Ghost, Stave 1) — the novel's
  thesis statement about social responsibility, spoken before Scrooge has
  changed.
- *"I will honour Christmas in my heart, and try to keep it all the year"*
  (Scrooge, Stave 5) — his transformation made explicit.
- *"God bless Us, Every One!"* (Tiny Tim) — associates innocence and
  generosity with the novel's moral centre.

## Context worth using

Dickens wrote A Christmas Carol in 1843, partly in response to reports on
child labour and after visiting a "ragged school" for poor children. The
**Poor Law Amendment Act (1834)** pushed poor people toward workhouses,
which Dickens viewed as cruel; Scrooge's early dialogue directly echoes
attitudes Dickens is criticising. Using this accurately shows AO3 — again,
as an explanation of a choice, not a bolted-on fact.

> **Exam tip:** As with Macbeth, answer on both the extract and the whole
> novel. Use the extract as your anchor, then widen out with clear whole-
> novel references (not just one other quote dropped in).

> **Exam tip:** Dickens structures the novel around five staves (chapters)
> each built around a visitation — thinking about *where* a scene sits in
> that structure (which ghost, what Scrooge has/hasn't learned yet) often
> strengthens an answer.
