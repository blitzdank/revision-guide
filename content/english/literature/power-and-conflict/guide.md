## The exam question

Paper 2, Section B gives you **one poem printed in full** from the Power
and Conflict cluster, and asks you to compare it to **one other poem from
the cluster, chosen by you** (30 marks, no separate AO4 here). You need to
know all 15 poems well enough to pick a strong comparison quickly under
time pressure — not just your favourites.

## The 15 poems

| Poem | Poet | Roughly about |
|---|---|---|
| Ozymandias | Percy Bysshe Shelley | The ruins of a tyrant's statue — power is temporary, however great it felt at the time |
| London | William Blake | The suffering Blake sees walking through London — poverty, child labour, institutional control |
| The Prelude (extract) | William Wordsworth | A boy's awe and fear after a solo boat trip, confronted by nature's power |
| My Last Duchess | Robert Browning | A duke's chilling monologue revealing his controlling, murderous jealousy over his late wife |
| The Charge of the Light Brigade | Alfred Lord Tennyson | A doomed cavalry charge — soldiers' courage and loyalty despite a fatal command error |
| Storm on the Island | Seamus Heaney | A community's physical and psychological vulnerability to a storm |
| Bayonet Charge | Ted Hughes | A soldier's disorientation and fear in the chaos of a charge |
| Remains | Simon Armitage | A soldier haunted by guilt after shooting a looter |
| Poppies | Jane Weir | A mother's grief and worry for a son who has gone to war |
| War Photographer | Carol Ann Duffy | A photographer processing images of war's suffering, and the public's indifference |
| Tissue | Imtiaz Dharker | Using paper as a metaphor for how fragile power and human structures really are |
| The Émigrée | Carol Rumens | A speaker's idealised memory of a homeland she can't return to |
| Kamikaze | Beatrice Garland | A pilot's choice to turn back from a suicide mission, and its lasting consequences |
| Exposure | Wilfred Owen | Soldiers suffering and dying from cold and weather, as much as from the enemy |
| Checking Out Me History | John Agard | Reclaiming erased Black history against a Eurocentric education |

## Common comparison pairings

Some pairings come up often because they share a clear angle to compare:

- **Ozymandias + My Last Duchess** — both use a powerful man's own voice/
  legacy against him; both explore how power is performed and how fragile
  it really is.
- **Exposure + Bayonet Charge** or **Storm on the Island** — nature/weather
  as a hostile, uncontrollable force, rivalling human conflict itself.
- **War Photographer + Remains** — the lasting psychological impact of
  conflict on someone who witnessed or took part in it.
- **Poppies + Kamikaze** — conflict's impact on family relationships and
  on those left behind or changed by a choice.
- **Checking Out Me History + London** — a speaker protesting against an
  unjust system or structure of power.

> **Exam tip:** Don't just write about Poem A, then Poem B. Compare
> *throughout*, using connectives like "similarly," "in contrast,"
> "whereas," paragraph by paragraph.

> **Exam tip:** Compare at the level of ideas (what is each poem arguing
> about power or conflict?), form/structure (how is each poem shaped?) and
> language (what techniques does each poet use?) — a one-dimensional
> comparison (just language, say) won't reach the top band.

## A note on quotations

Most of these poems are still in copyright, so revision material here
refers to themes and techniques without reproducing the poems' text.
Always check the poem itself (your anthology/textbook) for exact wording
before using a quotation in an essay.
