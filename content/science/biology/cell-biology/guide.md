## Eukaryotic vs prokaryotic cells

- **Eukaryotic cells** (animal and plant cells) have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
- **Prokaryotic cells** (bacteria) are smaller, have no nucleus (DNA floats free as a single loop, sometimes with extra small rings called plasmids), and have a simpler structure.

**Animal cells** have: nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria, ribosomes.
**Plant cells** have all of the above, **plus**: a cell wall (made of cellulose), chloroplasts (for photosynthesis), and a permanent vacuole (filled with cell sap).

## Specialised cells

Cells **differentiate** to become specialised for a specific job — structure matches function:
- **Sperm cells**: a tail (flagellum) for swimming, lots of mitochondria for energy.
- **Root hair cells**: a large surface area for absorbing water and minerals.
- **Nerve cells**: long, with branched connections for transmitting electrical signals.

## Microscopy and magnification

$$\text{magnification} = \frac{\text{size of image}}{\text{size of real object}}$$

Rearrange as needed: actual size = image size ÷ magnification; image size = actual size × magnification.

**Example:** an image of a cell measures 5 mm wide, and the magnification used was ×500. Find the actual size.

$$\text{actual size} = \frac{5 \text{ mm}}{500} = 0.01 \text{ mm} = 10 \text{ μm}$$

> **Exam tip:** Watch your units carefully — cells are usually measured in **micrometres (μm)**, not millimetres, so you often need to convert (1 mm = 1000 μm) as a final step.

**Required practical — using a light microscope:** you should be able to set up a microscope, observe plant and animal cells, and produce a labelled scientific drawing **with a magnification or scale given**. Know how to calculate magnification from a scale bar, and the order of objective lenses (low power first, to locate the specimen, then increase magnification).

## Cell division

- The **cell cycle** has growth/DNA-replication stages followed by **mitosis** (the nucleus divides to produce two genetically identical cells) and cytokinesis (the cell itself splits).
- Mitosis is used for growth and repair in the body.
- **Stem cells** are undifferentiated cells that can become different specialised cell types. Embryonic stem cells can become almost any cell type; adult stem cells (e.g. in bone marrow) are more limited.

## Transport in and out of cells

- **Diffusion**: the net movement of particles from an area of **higher** concentration to an area of **lower** concentration, down a concentration gradient — no energy needed.
- **Osmosis**: a special case of diffusion — the movement of **water** molecules across a partially permeable membrane, from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution.
- **Active transport**: movement of particles from a **lower** to a **higher** concentration (against the gradient) — this requires energy from respiration, unlike diffusion and osmosis.

**Required practical — osmosis in plant tissue:** investigate the effect of different sugar/salt solution concentrations on the mass of plant tissue (e.g. potato chips). Measure mass before and after soaking, then calculate percentage change:

$$\% \text{ change in mass} = \frac{\text{final mass} - \text{initial mass}}{\text{initial mass}} \times 100$$

A **positive** percentage change means the tissue gained water (so the solution outside was *more dilute* than inside the cells); a **negative** change means it lost water (the solution outside was *more concentrated*).

> **Exam tip:** For this practical, always control variables like the size/surface area of the tissue pieces and the time soaked — otherwise you can't fairly compare results across different concentrations.
