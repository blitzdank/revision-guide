## The structure of the atom

| Particle | Relative mass | Relative charge | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | 1 | +1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | 1 | 0 | Nucleus |
| Electron | very small (≈1/1836) | −1 | Shells (energy levels) around the nucleus |

- **Atomic number** = number of protons (= number of electrons, in a neutral atom).
- **Mass number** = number of protons + neutrons.
- Atoms are neutral overall because the number of protons equals the number of electrons.

## How the model of the atom developed

Science's understanding changed as new evidence came in — a good topic for "how did a scientific idea develop" questions:

1. Early model: atoms as solid, indivisible spheres.
2. **Plum pudding model**: following the discovery of the electron, atoms were pictured as a ball of positive charge with negative electrons embedded in it.
3. **Nuclear model**: the **alpha particle scattering experiment** — most alpha particles passed straight through a thin gold foil, but a small number bounced back or deflected at large angles. This showed most of the atom is empty space, with a tiny, dense, positively charged **nucleus**.
4. **Bohr's model**: electrons were shown to exist in **specific shells (energy levels)** at set distances from the nucleus, not just anywhere.
5. Later experiments identified the nucleus contains **protons** and (separately) **neutrons**.

> **Exam tip:** For "describe how the model changed" questions, name the key evidence that *caused* each change (especially the alpha scattering experiment), not just the sequence of models.

## Isotopes and relative atomic mass

**Isotopes** are atoms of the same element (same number of protons) with a **different number of neutrons**.

**Relative atomic mass** is a weighted average, accounting for the proportion of each isotope:

$$A_r = \frac{\sum (\text{isotope mass} \times \text{abundance})}{\text{total abundance}}$$

**Example:** chlorine is 75% chlorine-35 and 25% chlorine-37. Find the relative atomic mass.

$$A_r = \frac{(35 \times 75) + (37 \times 25)}{100} = \frac{2625 + 925}{100} = \frac{3550}{100} = 35.5$$

## Electronic structure

Electrons fill shells from the **innermost shell outward**, in this order: shell 1 holds up to 2 electrons; shells 2 and 3 hold up to 8 each (for the first 20 elements).

**Example:** sodium (atomic number 11) has electronic structure **2, 8, 1** (2 in the first shell, 8 in the second, 1 in the third/outer shell).

> **Exam tip:** The number of electrons in the **outer shell** is what determines an element's chemical properties and its group number on the periodic table — always check this when identifying a group from an electronic structure.

## The periodic table

Mendeleev's early periodic table left gaps for undiscovered elements and sometimes ordered elements by properties rather than strict atomic weight — these gaps were later filled, confirming his approach.

- **Group 0 (noble gases)**: full outer shells, so very **unreactive**; this is why they exist as stable single atoms.
- **Group 1 (alkali metals)**: one electron in the outer shell, so very reactive; **reactivity increases down the group** (the outer electron is further from the nucleus, so less strongly held, easier to lose).
- **Group 7 (halogens)**: seven electrons in the outer shell; **reactivity decreases down the group** (harder to gain one more electron as atoms get bigger); can displace a less reactive halogen from a compound (a **displacement reaction**).
